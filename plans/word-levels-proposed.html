Proposed · 2026-10-06

One new rule per level

A proposed cumulative word course for the Reading Learner app. Each small level adds one focus; earlier patterns can appear again.

41 small levels · 240 focus words · usually 6 words per level

Reviewable curriculum map only. The running app still uses its existing ten word levels.

See the artwork for all 41 levels

A practice round

3 familiar wordsThree familiar words, drawn from the child's demonstrated known-word bank.
4 focus encountersFour encounters from the current level's focus bank. Rotate the bank across rounds; the whole level need not fit in one round.
3 familiar wordsThree familiar words again. Repeats of known words are allowed.

Only the focus bank determines level completion. Keep the current requirement of two qualifying first-attempt Yes confirmations per focus word. A helped repeat advances the encounter but does not earn a qualifying first-attempt Yes.

Opening and closing words do not add new completion requirements. Preserve existing word trophies and earned confirmations. An already mastered focus word keeps its credit.

Check the starting point

  • The child recognises the ordinary single-letter consonant sounds, including hard c and g, consonant y, and x as ks.
  • The child can attempt blending simple consonant-vowel-consonant words.
  • These are provisional assumptions, not confirmed assessment results. Check them before adopting the course.

Possible familiar starters: mat, dad, hat.

Use these only if the child already reads them independently. Choose and confirm the familiar starter bank before the first practice round. Do not label an unseen word an easy win merely because it is decodable.

If these starting skills are not ready

Confirm starting skills and a familiar starter bank before enabling this course. If the prerequisite is not met, address the foundation explicitly rather than presenting unfamiliar words as wins.

The proposed levels

The sequence is cumulative: every level assumes the patterns from all earlier levels. Listed prerequisites highlight additional earlier patterns inside its focus words; they are not a separate route through the course.

Bold words practise the level’s focus. Familiar-word candidates are optional; use them only after he knows them.

Each letter name is spoken slowly on its own, with a short pause. The lesson then demonstrates the recorded phonics sound and whole-word examples. The U-name demonstration uses the real word you. Blending lessons play the separate consonant sounds before the whole word.

                                        What happens to the old words?

                                        95 existing words are in these banks; 145 words are new. 191 existing words are parked outside this core sequence. Their progress records would be preserved.

                                        Some later topics need separate levels: ea and further vowel spellings; u-e saying oo and coalescing sounds; open syllables and final y; soft c and g, ph and wh; silent consonants and further consonant teams; unstressed vowels and longer words; common exception words; ambiguous pronunciations.

                                        Deferred topics and reasons
                                        • ea and further vowel spellings: Separate spelling cohorts need their own levels; ee and ea should not share a new-rule level.
                                        • u-e saying oo and coalescing sounds: Keep them separate from the yoo cohort.
                                        • Open syllables and final y: baby combines an open syllable, final y and more than one syllable.
                                        • Soft c and g, ph and wh: Introduce each pattern separately after the core course.
                                        • Silent consonants and further consonant teams: They are additional correspondences, outside this first map.
                                        • Unstressed vowels and longer words: music and longer words require more than the core single-syllable patterns.
                                        • Common exception words: Words such as the, of, was and have need deliberate separate teaching. Teaching quiet th and buzzy th does not cover the exceptional vowel in the.
                                        • Ambiguous pronunciations: live and similar words need context or an explicitly selected meaning before they are suitable focus items.

                                        Review before adoption

                                        • One new focus means one unfamiliar spelling pattern or blending skill. A word may use several earlier patterns.
                                        • Every candidate still needs a child's-eye check for pronunciation and meaning. The word lists do not certify teaching quality.
                                        • The focus labels describe these word cohorts, not universal rules for every English word.
                                        • Review the starting assumptions and the first five banks before adopting the proposed course. No extra reference-audio recordings are required.

                                        Keep the current record → expected word → Got it? → Yes or record again flow. The voice-recognition experiment is not part of this proposal.

                                        Start the new course at the first unfinished focus bank using preserved word progress. Let a parent choose Level 1 for practice without wiping trophies.
